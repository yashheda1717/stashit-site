How to compress a PDF to 100 KB on your phone

The upload box says 100 KB and your PDF is 2 MB. Here is how to shrink it on your phone without making it unreadable.

By the Stash-it! teamUpdated 12 October 20264 min read

Many online portals cap uploads at a small size: 100 KB, 200 KB, 300 KB or 500 KB are commonly seen limits. A single phone scan can easily be 1 or 2 MB. The good news is that most PDFs can be made far smaller without becoming hard to read, if you know where the size comes from.

Limits differ from form to form, and some forms also limit the number of pages or ask for colour scans. Check the instructions on your form before you compress.

Why PDFs from a phone are so big

A PDF made from a phone scan is really a set of photos wrapped in a PDF. The size depends on three things:

  • Number of pages: every page is another image.
  • Colour: a full-colour page takes much more space than a black-and-white one.
  • Resolution and quality: modern phone cameras capture far more detail than a small upload needs.

So the fastest wins come from fewer pages, less colour and a sensible resolution, before any compression tool even runs.

Step 1: Prepare a lean PDF

  1. Keep only the pages the form needs. If the portal wants the first page of a certificate, do not upload all four. Remove blank pages and backs of single-sided documents.
  2. Scan in black and white or greyscale for typed documents, unless the form asks for colour. This alone can cut the size dramatically.
  3. Use a document scanner, not the normal camera. A scanner crops the page, flattens it and whitens the background, and a white background compresses much better than a grey, shadowy one.
  4. Avoid screenshots of PDFs. Work from the original scan or file.

Step 2: Compress to the target size

Now use a compression tool. The most useful ones let you type the target size, such as 100 KB, rather than choosing a vague "low, medium, high". Aim a little under the limit, for example 90 KB for a 100 KB limit, to allow for any rounding by the portal.

After compressing, always:

  • Open the new PDF and zoom into the smallest text, such as a roll number or date.
  • Check that stamps, seals and signatures are still visible.
  • Confirm the file size in your phone's file manager.

If the text has turned to mush, go back a step: reduce the page count or switch to black and white, then compress again with a less aggressive target.

Still too big? Try these

Many pages and a tiny limitCheck whether the portal accepts separate uploads per document, or only the key pages. Split the PDF if needed.
Colour certificate with a sealKeep colour but crop tightly to the page edges; compress to just under the limit.
ID card front and backPut both sides on one page instead of two pages.
Photo saved as PDFResize the photo first, then convert it to PDF.

Too small can be a problem too

Some forms set a minimum size as well, or reject files where the text is unreadable. If a portal says "between 50 KB and 100 KB", a 12 KB file may fail. Compress to the range, not as low as possible.

A word on online compressors

Many websites will compress a PDF for you, but that means uploading your documents to someone else's server. For certificates, ID proofs and bank papers, it is safer to use a tool that works on your phone, so the file never leaves it.
