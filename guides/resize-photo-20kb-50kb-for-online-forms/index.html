How to resize a photo to 20 KB or 50 KB for online forms

Online forms are strict about photo and signature sizes. Here is how to hit the exact KB limit on your phone and keep the picture clear.

By the Stash-it! teamUpdated 12 October 20265 min read

Exam registrations, job applications, scholarship portals and many government services ask you to upload a photo and a signature within a tight size limit. Upload a 3 MB phone photo and the form rejects it. Shrink it carelessly and it comes out blurry or the wrong shape. This guide explains what the form is really asking for and how to get it right on your phone.

Every form sets its own rules. Sizes such as 20 KB or 50 KB are commonly asked, but always read the instructions on the form you are filling and follow those exactly.

KB, pixels and centimetres are three different things

Most confusion comes from mixing these up. A form may give you one, two or all three:

  • File size (KB): how much space the file takes, for example "between 20 KB and 50 KB". This is about compression and quality.
  • Dimensions (pixels): the width and height of the image, for example "200 × 230 pixels". This is about shape and resolution.
  • Print size (cm or mm): how big the photo is meant to look when printed, for example "3.5 cm × 4.5 cm". On a screen this mostly decides the shape (the ratio of width to height).

Get the shape right first, then the pixel dimensions, then squeeze the file into the KB range. Doing it in that order keeps the photo sharp.

Before you start: take a good photo

A clean source makes everything easier. Stand facing a window for soft light, in front of a plain light wall. Hold the phone at eye level, look straight at the camera and keep a neutral expression unless the form says otherwise. For a signature, sign on plain white paper with a dark pen, and photograph it flat in good light with no shadow from your hand or phone.

Step-by-step: resize to the size the form asks for

  1. Read the requirements. Note the KB range, any pixel dimensions, the file type (usually JPG or JPEG) and whether a minimum size is mentioned. A file can be rejected for being too small as well as too big.
  2. Crop to the right shape. For a photo, crop so your face and the top of your shoulders fill the frame as the form describes. For a signature, crop tightly around the signature with a small white margin.
  3. Set the dimensions, if given. If the form gives pixel dimensions, resize to exactly those. If it does not, a few hundred pixels on each side is usually plenty for a small upload.
  4. Compress to the KB range. Use a tool that lets you type a target such as 50 KB or 20 KB. Aim slightly under the maximum, for example 45 KB for a 50 KB limit, to leave a safety margin.
  5. Save as JPG unless the form asks for another format. PNG files are usually much larger for photos.
  6. Check the result. Open the file, zoom in and make sure the face or signature is clear. Check the file size in your file manager.

Common size requests you may see

These are examples, not rules. Your form's instructions always win.

Mistakes that get uploads rejected

  • Stretching instead of cropping. Forcing a tall photo into square dimensions squashes your face. Crop to the right shape first.
  • Over-compressing. A 50 KB limit does not mean you need a 5 KB file. Very small files look blocky and may be rejected by staff even if the portal accepts them.
  • Screenshots of photos. A screenshot adds the phone's screen edges and changes the size unpredictably. Work from the original photo.
  • Wrong file type. HEIC or WebP images may not be accepted. Save as JPG.
  • Swapping photo and signature. It happens more than you would think. Name the files clearly, for example photo_50kb.jpg and sign_20kb.jpg.

What if the form wants a PDF?

Some portals ask for certificates or ID proofs as a PDF under a size limit, often 100 KB, 200 KB or 500 KB. The approach is similar but there are extra tricks, such as scanning in black and white and removing extra pages. Final checklist before you upload

  • The file size is inside the range the form gives, with a small margin
  • The dimensions match, if the form specifies them
  • The face or signature is sharp when you zoom in
  • The file is a JPG (or whatever format the form asks for)
  • The photo and signature files are clearly named and not swapped
